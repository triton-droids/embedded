# humanoid_full_body_description

Full-body humanoid URDF: the upper body plus the articulated lower body at the nominal five-hole pelvis mount.
27 links: 22 body links, 20 actuated body joints, plus the pan-tilt head (5 links, 2 joints, see "Pan-tilt head"). Fixed base at `torso_link`.

Two variants with identical kinematics and inertials, a light **simplified** model (default) and the original
**detailed** CAD meshes. See [MODELS.md](MODELS.md) for the comparison and when to use which.

> **Orientation fix (2026-11-22).** The CAD robot faces −y. Until this fix the model kept the CAD frame, matched the legs
> to the tracking policy's +y-forward leg model, turned both feet 180° and shifted the leg zeros toward the policy. Front
> and back were reversed: the feet, the knee bend and the hip range were all mirrored. `scripts/reorient.py` rebuilds the
> model from commit 77cf784c with the original feet and the torso frame turned 180°, so it faces +y, and re-derives every
> joint sign. Sections below marked *(before the orientation fix)* report results on the reversed model. Their
> conclusions about the hip stop, the squat depth and the get-up no longer hold.

## Layout

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| [`urdf/full_body.urdf.xacro`](urdf/full_body.urdf.xacro) | **Source of truth**, about 200 lines: one `cad_link` and one joint macro call per link/joint, grouped by torso / pelvis / arms / legs |
| `urdf/full_body.urdf` (installed only) | Generated at build time by CMake (`xacro`), so consumers keep loading `share/humanoid_full_body_description/urdf/full_body.urdf` |
| `meshes/visual/<link>.stl` | The link's CAD parts, each decimated separately and then concatenated (427k triangles in total). Each part stays within 1 mm of the original (95th percentile); the torso is held to 2 mm so it fits under MuJoCo's 200k-face STL limit. Simple parts such as the two torso battery packs are kept unchanged |
| `meshes/collision/<link>.stl` | One convex piece per CAD part, concatenated (about 2,500 pieces, 140k triangles): screws/nuts (< 5 cm³) as an oriented box, ordinary parts as their convex hull, strongly concave parts (brackets, extrusions) split with CoACD. See "Collision model" below |
| `cad_source/` | Original per-part export: `full_body_parts.urdf` (1,086 `<visual>`/`<collision>` parts, 3.06M triangles) and its STLs. Installed as the **detailed** model `urdf/full_body_detailed.urdf` (CMake option `INSTALL_DETAILED_MODEL`, default ON) |
| `config/motors_full_body.yaml`, `urdf/full_body_wbc*.urdf` | Generated by [`scripts/make_sim_mjcf.py`](scripts/make_sim_mjcf.py) with `mjcf/full_body_sim.xml`: the 18-motor simulation registry and the `walking_controller` models (arms fixed / all 20 joints). See "Model-based walking" |
| [`scripts/simplify_meshes.py`](scripts/simplify_meshes.py) | Regenerates `meshes/` from `cad_source/` |

Before simplifying, the robot had 1,086 separate meshes with 3M triangles. RViz, move_group and MuJoCo each
needed 2–3 GB, and two copies running at once took down a 15 GB desktop. Now the whole MoveIt + MuJoCo stack
uses about 1 GB. Joint origins, axes and inertials are unchanged: link frames and the CAD inertials were kept,
and only the mesh files were replaced. The colours are simplified to one material per link.

Both models have identical links, joints and inertials. Pick one with
`ros2 launch humanoid_full_body_moveit_config inspect.launch.py model:=simplified|detailed`. The detailed model
needs about 2 GB for move_group and about 2.5 GB for MuJoCo.

Regenerate the meshes after a CAD change. These tools are offline only, not runtime dependencies:

```bash
python3 -m venv /tmp/meshenv
/tmp/meshenv/bin/pip install trimesh==4.4.9 fast-simplification==0.1.9 rtree==1.3.0 coacd==1.0.14 scipy
cd humanoid_full_body_description && OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 /tmp/meshenv/bin/python scripts/simplify_meshes.py
```

Then regenerate the MoveIt self-collision matrix (see the MoveIt config README), because the collision shapes changed.

## Collision model

Same idea as the SO-101 work in `lerobot_test`: convex shapes per *part*, not per link. A single hull per link filled the gaps
between parts (the pelvis became a solid block) and wrongly rejected 1.7–4% of random poses. Compared against the detailed
per-part meshes in MoveIt over 900 random poses (±0.8, ±1.6 and ±π rad):

| Collision model | False positives (only the simplified model collides) | Missed collisions |
|---|---|---|
| One convex hull per link (previous) | 4.0% / 1.7% / 3.0% | 0 |
| One hull per part | 3.7% / 1.0% / 0.7% | 0 |
| **Per part + CoACD on concave parts (current)** | **0.7% / 0% / 0.3%** | **0** |

CoACD is approximate: with each piece capped at 64 vertices, a decomposed part's surface can lie up to 2.6 mm outside its pieces
(the script prints this per part). No missed collision showed up in the comparison, but treat contacts closer than ~3 mm as
unresolved. MoveIt's `default_robot_padding` does not apply to self-collision, so it cannot close this gap. One part
(`lower_body_link_002_001`) crashes CoACD on every seed and keeps its plain hull. MuJoCo convexifies each collision STL as a whole,
so in MuJoCo every link is still a single hull.

## Frames

`pelvis_link` is fixed to `torso_link` (`torso_to_pelvis`). The pelvis top face sits flush against the torso
bottom (z = 0), and the hip-pitch axes are exactly ±x. Torso and pelvis together form the reference frame for
the joint zero below.

The STL names in `cad_source/meshes/` are unique across all four folders. Names that clashed carry a
`left_arm_` / `right_arm_` / `lower_body_` prefix. MuJoCo's URDF importer names mesh assets by file basename
and silently reuses the first mesh loaded under a duplicate name. Before the rename, the pelvis rendered as
torso parts and the right arm as left-arm parts.

For MoveIt 2 / RViz2 inspection, see [humanoid_full_body_moveit_config](../humanoid_full_body_moveit_config/README.md).

## Joint zero

At `q = 0` the robot stands upright: legs straight, knee axes lateral, hip-yaw axes vertical, soles flat,
arms hanging straight down. In `torso_link`, +z is up and +y is forward (the toes point +y); the `left_*` joints are on
the −x side, the robot's left. `torso_link` is the CAD torso frame turned 180° about z, because the CAD export faces −y.
In the source CAD, the legs were posed rather than straight. The right knee was bent about 29°, the left leg
was yawed about 33° and pitched about 20°, and the right hip was rolled about 8°. These offsets were moved into
the `rpy` of the 10 leg joint origins, so that the old pose equals the new joint values below:

| Joint | Old pose in new coordinates (deg) |
|---|---|
| left hip1 / hip2 / thigh / knee / ankle | 22.1 / −1.5 / 32.5 / −31.8 / 5.5 |
| right hip1 / hip2 / thigh / knee / ankle | −2.1 / 8.8 / −0.9 / 29.7 / 2.2 |

Link geometry and joint positions are unchanged. Only the child frames are rotated about each joint axis.
The arm joints were already at a sensible zero (hanging down), so they were not changed.

## Pan-tilt head

An STS3215 + RealSense D435 pan-tilt head sits on the two top 1010 rails. Both URDFs carry the same block between the
`HEAD BEGIN/END` markers. It is written by `mechanical/sts3215_d435_pantilt_dual_horn/export_full_body_head.py`
(`--layout sim-experimental`), which also writes the head meshes. Rerun it rather than editing the block.

```text
torso_link
└── head_base_link        head_mount_joint (fixed): x 0, y 0.0324, z 0.3914 (adapter plate on the rails,
    │                     tray front flush with the front rail)
    └── neck_yaw_link     neck_yaw_joint, z axis, ±100°, + turns the camera to the robot's left
        └── neck_pitch_link   neck_pitch_joint, x axis, ±75°, + tilts the camera up (axis 0.141 m above the plate)
            ├── camera_link   D435 depth origin, ROS convention (x forward, y left, z up)
            └── head_imu_link
```

- 0.605 kg. Printed parts are PETG at 60 % effective density; servos, camera and bearings use catalogue masses.
- One visual mesh per material (off-white print, black servos, orange horns, aluminium D435, green board, steel
  bearings) and one collision mesh per link.
- `camera_link` is the last camera frame in the URDF; the RealSense driver publishes the optical frames under it.
- `make_sim_mjcf.py` welds `neck_yaw_joint` and `neck_pitch_joint` in the MJCF and in both WBC models (no STS3215 motor
  model yet), so the simulation keeps 18 motors and the controller models their joint counts.
- The SRDF has 79 head pairs from `collisions_updater` (3 adjacent, 76 never); the body pairs are unchanged.

## Mass properties (from the CAD export, not measured)

Total 32.5 kg: head 0.6, torso 9.0, pelvis 3.5, each arm 3.3, each leg 6.4. Height at the zero pose 1.05 m (sole to torso top,
without the head). The 31.9 kg in the test results below is the model before the head was added.

The shoulder mounts (`base_link_333` / `base_link_314`, the three-lobed brackets bolted to the torso frame) were changed
from 6061 aluminium to printed ASA, which made the robot 1.0 kg lighter (−500 g each, 652 → 152 g). The torso mass, COM and inertia were recomputed
from the two parts' geometry (density difference times the part's own volume integrals, then moved to the new COM with the parallel-axis theorem):
mass 10.00 → 9.00 kg, COM z 0.192 → 0.181 m, ixx/iyy/izz 0.148/0.275/0.162 → 0.133/0.231/0.129 kg·m².
The inner round part at each shoulder (`base_link_332` / `313`) is the shoulder motor, not the mount, and was not changed.

The ball and block at the end of each forearm (`link_004_004`, 209 cm³, and `link_004_003`, 142 cm³) were exported as steel; they are
printed PLA at 20% infill. They alone made up 2.75 of the forearm's 3.19 kg. Effective density = 1.24 g/cm³ × (solid shell + 20% of
the core), with the shell taken as surface area × 1.2 mm (3 perimeters / top-bottom layers): 0.44 g/cm³ for the block (63 g) and
0.41 g/cm³ for the ball (85 g). Same delta method as the torso (the forearm's CAD inertial matches its geometry to 2 mm): each forearm
3.19 → 0.59 kg, COM z −0.098 → −0.058 m. Each arm 5.9 → 3.3 kg.
For a robot about 1.13 m tall without a head this is on the heavy side. For comparison, a 1.3 m commercial humanoid weighs about 35 kg.
Every inertia tensor is positive definite, satisfies the triangle inequality, and has its COM inside the link's hull,
so the values are physically consistent. Some values still need checking. Weigh the real parts before using these
for dynamics or control:

| Link | Mass | Implied density (mass / CAD volume) | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| `*_link_004` (forearm) | 0.59 kg (was 3.19) | 1.4 g/cm³ | Ball and block corrected from steel to PLA, 20% infill (85 + 63 g). Weigh one to confirm |
| `*_link_002` | 0.024 kg | 8.7 g/cm³ | Tiny coaxial disc between the two yaw joints. Brass/copper density |
| `*_link_001` | 0.73 kg | 4.9 g/cm³ | Mixed steel parts |
| `torso_link` | 9.0017 kg (was 10.0016) | 3.0 g/cm³ | Includes the two battery packs (170×60×46 mm). The original 10.0016 kg looked like a manual round number; the ASA shoulder-mount change is applied on top of it. Weighing the real torso (without pelvis) would settle it |
| legs, pelvis | 0.57–3.5 kg | 2.1–2.9 g/cm³ | Close to aluminium (2.7). Motors are probably not at their real density, so the leg masses may be low |

With the forearm correction each arm (3.3 kg) is about half of each leg (6.4 kg).
With that mass at the end of each arm, arm motions shift the whole-body COM noticeably, which matters for balance control.

## Joint names, signs and limits

The leg joints use the motor registry names. Every joint sign keeps the registry's meaning and was checked by forward
kinematics (`scripts/reorient.py`). **Fusion shows the opposite sign for hip pitch and ankle.**

| Leg joint (was) | Name | Positive | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `*_pelvis_hip_connector_joint` | `*_hip1_joint` (hip pitch) | flexion (foot forward) | left −17.5 / right −15° (extension stop, see below) .. +120° |
| `*_hip_joint` | `*_hip2_joint` (hip roll) | left − / right + abducts | left −90..25°, right −25..90° |
| `*_thigh_joint` | `*_thigh_joint` (hip yaw) | toe outward | ±45° |
| `*_shank_joint` | `*_knee_joint` | flexion is **negative** | −120..0° |
| `*_foot_joint` | `*_ankle_joint` (ankle pitch) | toe up | −120 (toe down) .. +96.6° (toe up), measured; the four-bar drives about −65..+75° (`config/motors_full_body.yaml`) |

The arms are renamed to `*_shoulder_pitch_joint` (was `*_shoulder_abduction`), `*_shoulder_roll_joint` (`*_joint_001_10`),
`*_shoulder_yaw_joint` (`*_joint_002_16`), `*_upper_arm_twist_joint` (`*_joint_003_45`, coaxial with the yaw) and
`*_elbow_joint` (`*_joint_004_52`). Shoulder pitch + swings the arm forward, shoulder roll + abducts, and elbow + flexes
(assumed forward, 0..130° from Fusion).

## Ankle four-bar

The ankle motor sits in the shank, 166 mm above the ankle axis. A crank on its output pushes a rod down to a lever at
the heel. The CAD export put crank and rod in the shank link, so they stood still while the foot rotated and the rod
came off the foot. `scripts/ankle_linkage.py` gives them their own links:

| Link | Parent, joint | Moves with |
|---|---|---|
| `*_ankle_crank_link` (crank, hub and pin hardware, 23–26 g) | shank, `*_ankle_crank_joint` about the motor axis | the motor |
| `*_ankle_rod_link` (pushrod, 30 g) | crank, `*_ankle_rod_joint` about the crank pin | the crank pin |
| `*_ankle_rod_end` (frame, no mass) | rod, fixed at the lower eye | on the heel lever pin |

A URDF tree cannot hold the closed loop, so both joints are `<mimic>` joints of `*_ankle_joint` (RViz, MoveIt and
`robot_state_publisher` move them). A mimic is linear: over the driven range −65..+75° it is within 0.9° (crank) and
1.6° (rod), so in RViz the rod end may miss the heel pin by up to about 5 mm. The simulation closes the loop exactly:
`mjcf/full_body_sim.xml` keeps crank and rod as passive hinges and pins the rod end to the foot with an equality
`connect` (gap under 1.5 mm while the ankle sweeps at 1.4 rad/s, 0 at rest). The Pinocchio models (WBC) weld crank and
rod to the shank. The MoveIt MuJoCo backend (`description/humanoid_full_body_moveit_config/scripts/mujoco_sim.py`) follows the
mimic relation with joint equalities.

Pin positions measured from the CAD (cylinder axes of the crank hub, crank pin and rod eyes), both legs:

| Bar | CAD | `ankle_mapping` (motors.yaml) |
|---|---|---|
| ground: ankle axis to motor axis | 166.24 mm | 6.5625 in = 166.69 mm |
| crank (`L2`, on the motor) | 45.00 mm | 1.875 in = 47.63 mm |
| rod (`L3`) | 166.29 mm | 6.5 in = 165.10 mm |
| heel lever (`L4`, on the foot) | 44.40 mm | 1.79 in = 45.47 mm |

`ankle_mapping` solves the Freudenstein equation with the ankle as θ2 and the motor as θ4, so its `L2` is the crank and
`L4` the heel lever. `ankle_mapping` with the CAD lengths reproduces the CAD column below to 0.1°.

Crank (= motor) angle for an ankle angle, four-bar solved with the CAD pins (left leg; right within 0.5° in the driven
range), against the motor angle `ankle_mapping` commands:

| Ankle | −65° | −45° | −20° | 0° | +20° | +45° | +75° | +90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crank, CAD | −63.4° | −44.2° | −19.7° | 0° | +19.7° | +44.2° | +72.6° | +82.9° |
| Motor, `ankle_mapping` | −55.9° | −39.8° | −17.0° | +1.9° | +21.1° | +45.1° | +72.9° | – |

Why they differ: in the CAD the four-bar is almost a parallelogram (crank 45.00 ≈ lever 44.40, rod 166.29 ≈ ground
166.24), so the motor turns almost exactly with the foot. The mapping's bars are not: its crank is 2.6 mm longer than
its lever and its rod 1.6 mm shorter than its ground bar. A longer crank needs less rotation for the same rod travel,
so the motor turns less than the foot, most at toe-down: 7.5° short at −65°, about equal at +75°. Its 90° zero
angles add about 2° (the CAD has the lever at 87.9° / 90.9° and the crank at 92.1° / 89.2° to the ground bar at ankle
0, left / right). This assumes `ankle_mapping`'s ankle angle has the URDF's sign (+ lifts the toe), not checked on the
robot. If the hardware is built like the CAD, the controller's ankle angle is off by that much. Check on the robot
(crank and lever pin distances with calipers, or the foot angle at a few motor angles) before changing `ankle_mapping`.

## MuJoCo simulation (humanoid_sim)

[`mjcf/full_body_sim.xml`](mjcf/full_body_sim.xml) is the robot for `humanoid_sim`:
`ros2 launch humanoid_sim sim.launch.py robot:=full_body` (the default `robot:=legs` keeps the policy's 16 kg leg model).
It is generated from the simplified URDF by [`scripts/make_sim_mjcf.py`](scripts/make_sim_mjcf.py). The script adds a floating
base (`floating_base` + `imu` site at the torso origin), the `top` site for the simulated head, sole boxes and sites
(`*_foot_collision_box`, `*_foot`), the policy MJCF's leg damping and armature, position servos that hold the arms at
ctrl = 0, and the SRDF's disabled collision pairs as exclusions. Rerun the script after any URDF change.

First test with the existing tracking policy (2026-11-21, headless, `auto_start:=true`; runs where startup tripped
`imu_freshness` before release are left out):

| Robot | Walking reference | Holding frame 0 (standing) |
|---|---|---|
| `legs` (policy MJCF, 16 kg) | stayed up for 37 s, 15–17 m | stayed up |
| `full_body` (31.9 kg) | fell sideways 1.6 s after release (2 of 2) | fell 1.6 s after release |
| `full_body` with torso and arms made nearly massless (16.3 kg) | fell 2.2 s after release (2 of 2), 1.2 m | – |

So the policy did not transfer, and **mass was not the only reason**. The full-body CAD legs differed from the policy MJCF.
With all joints at zero, each ankle sat 2–3 cm further out, the feet were 3.5 cm further apart, and the foot extended mostly
*behind* the ankle (sole centre 2 cm back) where the policy's foot extends mostly in front (2.5 cm forward).

**After that test, both feet were turned 180° about the vertical through the ankle** (the CAD foot was mounted the wrong way
round). The ankle axis is negated, so its world direction and sign convention are unchanged. Now the sole centre is 2.0–3.2 cm in
front of the hip, as in the policy MJCF (2.4 cm), and the COM sits at the same fore-aft place over the feet as in the 16 kg model.
Still different from the policy MJCF:
- Each ankle sits 2–3 cm further out.
- The two feet are the same, unmirrored part, so the left sole is 1.1 cm outboard of its ankle and the right one 1.4 cm inboard.
  The policy MJCF has both about 1 cm inboard.

Rerun after the foot fix (same policy and launch; runs where startup tripped `imu_freshness` were retried):

| Robot | Walking reference | Holding frame 0 (standing) |
|---|---|---|
| `legs` | no fall in 38 s, 14.6 m | – |
| `full_body` (31.9 kg) | fell after 2.0 / 2.2 / 2.5 s, 0.35–0.59 m, always rolling to +41° | fell after 1.7 / 1.7 s |
| `full_body` with near-massless torso and arms (16.3 kg) | fell after 3.1 / 3.1 / 4.1 s, 1.5–1.7 m | fell after 2.4 s |

The foot fix helps a little (+0.5–1 s), but the policy still does not carry over, even at 16 kg, and even when holding still.
The ankle sat 2–3 cm further out than in the policy MJCF.

**Leg zero matched to the policy MJCF.** The leg joint zeros were then shifted by at most 2.4° (left hip1 −2.4°, hip2 +1.5°,
knee +2.3°; right hip2 −1.0°, the rest under 0.5°), so that at q = 0 the ankle sits where the policy MJCF puts it, while the
sole stays nearly flat (tilt 1.5° / 0.95°). Without an ankle roll joint, both cannot hold exactly. The ankle is now 8.6–9.4 cm
out from hip1 (policy 7.8 cm), and its fore-aft offset dropped from 1.2 cm to under 1 mm. Retest:

| Robot | Walking reference | Holding frame 0 (standing) |
|---|---|---|
| `full_body` (31.9 kg) | fell after 2.1 / 2.3 / 2.5 s, 0.6–0.7 m; now pitching (−44°), no longer always rolling to one side | fell after 1.8 / 1.9 s |
| `full_body`, 16.3 kg variant | fell after 2.7 / 3.1 / 3.4 s, 1.3–1.7 m | fell after 3.2 s |

Matching the zero removed the one-sided roll but not the falls, so the leg zero was not the cause. What still differs from the
policy MJCF: each leg link's mass and inertia distribution, a 13% smaller sole (box half-sizes 3.5 × 9.6 cm against 4.0 × 10.5 cm),
the joint positions along the chain (for example, hip2 sits 1 cm further out), and, at full weight, the 15.6 kg upper body.
**The existing tracking policy does not run this robot; it needs a policy trained on this model.** Which CAD matches the built
robot still has to be checked on the hardware.
The SRDF collision matrix was regenerated after the fix: 21 adjacent pairs and 170 never colliding (the leg ranges exclude
more poses now).

## Model-based walking (WBC)

`walking_controller` (LIPM MPC + TSID, no RL; `controller:=model_walk`) runs the full-body robot with
`robot:=full_body`. `make_sim_mjcf.py` also writes its Pinocchio model, `urdf/full_body_wbc.urdf`. In that model the arm joints are fixed, so the
arms ride on the torso as rigid mass (31.9 kg in total), and the frames are named `torso`, `left_foot` and `right_foot`.
`full_body_wbc.dynamics.json` holds the identity base offset, the leg armature/damping, and the sole corners of the MJCF foot boxes.
The Pinocchio COM matches MuJoCo's.

```bash
scripts/sim_experimental.sh controller:=model_walk robot:=full_body auto_start:=true   # steps in place
ros2 topic pub --once /walk/step_length std_msgs/msg/Float64 "{data: 0.04}"           # walk forward
```

First results (2026-11-21, default walking_controller gains, tuned for the 16 kg leg model):

| Run | Stepping in place | Walking 0.04 m/step | 0.08 m/step | Standing still (no stepping) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offline (`walking_controller.offline`, fixed and Jetson-like timing) | 30 s, 55 steps; 20 s with chain timing | 30 s, 1.85 m | torso tilt fault at 11 s | tips back, fault at 6.7 s |
| ROS (`sim_experimental`, CycloneDDS) | 2 of 5 runs stepped for the whole 38–39 s; 3 faulted (torso tilt) 5–10 s after release, before any walk command | – | – | – |

So model-based control does keep the full-body robot up, which the tracking policy did not.

**The ROS-only failures were mostly a launch bug**, not the controller: `sim.yaml` sets `publish_rate_hz` and
`ground_truth_rate_hz` (50 Hz) under the node names `mujoco_sim` / `sim_motor_backend`, and a node-scoped value outranks
the launch's wildcard dict whatever the file order. So `controller:=model_walk`'s 200 Hz joint states and ground truth
were really 50 Hz, and the 200 Hz controller ran every 20 ms on 20 ms old samples (the offline harness runs at 200 Hz).
`sim.launch.py` now repeats its values node-scoped for every `sim.yaml` node that sets the same key. This affects every
robot and controller that relied on those launch overrides (`model_walk`, the WBC, the tuned PD profile, `minimal`).

### With the arms (18 motor joints, `robot:=full_body_arms`)

`robot:=full_body_arms` drives the arms with motors too:
- the simulator uses `config/motors_full_body.yaml`: the 10 leg motors of `motor_control_hybrid/config/motors.yaml`
  plus 8 arm motors: shoulder pitch (on the torso) and shoulder roll RS-03 (60 Nm), shoulder yaw and elbow RS-02 (17 Nm).
  The motor models are the hardware's; CAN ids, directions, zero offsets and gains (kp 40 / kd 2) are placeholders.
  `*_upper_arm_twist_joint` (coaxial with the shoulder yaw, 24 g link, no motor) is welded in the MJCF and the WBC models.
  (The first results below used 10 placeholder 17 Nm motors including the twist; the retest with the real motors is
  at the end of this section.)
  `humanoid_sim` replaces the MJCF's arm position servos with the same MIT-law motors as the legs.
- `walking_controller` loads `urdf/full_body_wbc_arms.urdf` (all 18 motor joints; the continuous arm joints become revolute
  ±π, since Pinocchio gives a continuous joint nq = 2).
- The WBC has an extra posture task on the arm joints only (`arm_posture_weight` 0.1). With just the global posture
  weight (0.01) the QP used the arms for the CoM and angular-momentum tasks. The legs have no ankle roll, so the shoulder
  roll is the only thing that moves the CoM sideways in double support. The shoulder roll ran to 1.15 rad and every ROS
  run failed at the first step.
- Arm swing: each shoulder pitch reference is `-4 rad/m ×` that side's foot offset ahead of the feet's midpoint, clipped
  to ±0.4 rad. The arm swings forward when the other foot is forward. Shoulder pitch + = arm forward on both sides.

```bash
scripts/sim_experimental.sh controller:=model_walk robot:=full_body_arms auto_start:=true
```

Results (2026-11-21; ROS = `sim_experimental`, 200 Hz after the fix above, stepping in place, then 0.04 m/step from 22 s):

| | Arms fixed (`full_body`) | Arms driven (`full_body_arms`) |
|---|---|---|
| ROS, 3–4 runs | 1 of 3 ran the whole 43 s; 2 faulted (torso tilt) 5–7 s after the walk command | **3 of 4** ran the whole 43 s (1.5–1.7 m); 1 faulted (torso tilt) 6 s after the walk command |
| Offline with ROS-chain timing, in place 12 s | – | 4 of 4 seeds (without the arm posture task: 1 of 3) |
| Offline with ROS-chain timing, 0.04 m/step 15 s | – | 2 of 2 seeds, 0.78 m |
| Offline 0.06 / 0.08 m/step | – | 0.06: 20 s, 1.74 m; 0.08: torso tilt fault at 10.5 s |

In the ROS runs the shoulder pitch swings over 0.36–0.51 rad.

### Sideways torso lean (fixed)

In those runs the torso leaned sideways: ±12–17° while stepping in place, and 10–25° to one side, on average +14°,
while walking. There were two causes:

1. **Torso orientation task too weak for a heavy upper body.** The legs have no ankle roll. With the 16 kg leg
   model's torso weight (0.5, kp 50 against a CoM weight of 10), the QP moved the CoM sideways by leaning the torso
   on the hip roll. The torso and arms are about half of the 32 kg. `walking_controller` now has `torso_kp` /
   `torso_weight` node parameters; the defaults are unchanged (50 / 0.5), and `sim.launch.py` sets 150 / 5 for
   `robot:=full_body*`. Offline with ROS-chain timing at 0.04 m/step, the largest lean went from 14.9° to 4.3°.
2. **Feet yaw drift while walking straight.** Each swing target copied the stance foot's measured yaw, so yaw slips
   added up. After 20 s both feet were 25–30° off the torso, which holds the heading, and the hip yaw was at
   ±0.5 rad. Straight steps now place the swing foot at the walking heading, and the drift stays at 8° or less.

After both fixes (ROS, 200 Hz, stepping in place, then 0.04 m/step from 22 s):

| `full_body_arms` | **4 of 4** | +0.7° / 4.2° | 1.42–1.53 m forward, ≤ 4 cm sideways |
| `full_body` (arms fixed) | 2 of 2 | – | – |

The run counts are small. Plain standing still drifts in COM (the ankle-only balance mode).

The tracking policy (RL) after the 200 Hz fix: the 16 kg leg model still walks (2 of 2 runs, 13–14 m in 38 s); the

### Squat and stand up (`walk_activity:=squat`)

```bash
scripts/sim_experimental.sh controller:=model_walk robot:=full_body_arms walk_activity:=squat   # walk_squat_depth_m:=0.07
```

After the start-up squat the robot squats `squat_depth_m` lower with both feet down, then stands up again
(`squat_cycles`, node default 1). The WBC alone, as in plain standing, could not hold the 32 kg robot with both feet
flat. Its contacts are soft tasks, so the QP planned leg accelerations that the closed chain cannot follow (hip yaw
−14 rad/s² planned, 0 in MuJoCo), and the CoM drifted back about 1 cm/s until the robot tipped over. The 16 kg leg
model does not have this problem. The squat therefore runs in joint space, like the start-up squat:
- inverse kinematics gives the deep posture: both feet fixed, the CoM over the same ground point and lower, and a
  low-weight torso orientation, so the torso leans forward and the arms reach forward as counterweights. With
  the torso upright the ankles (+34° limit) cannot keep the CoM over the feet. A joint at its limit is locked out of
  the IK step instead of clipped;
- the joints follow a min-jerk path between the two postures with stiff PD. The ankles need kp 100 / kd 4; at the
  start-up squat's 30 the robot tipped back on its first squat. The WBC torque, with the CoM on the same path, is the
  feedforward.

| Offline, depth 0.05–0.08 m (2 cycles each) | all pass; at 0.08 m the ankles reach 17 Nm; 0.10 m and deeper tip over |
| Offline with ROS-chain timing, 0.05 m | 2 of 2 seeds |
| ROS, 0.07 m (base 0.61 → 0.52 m, knee to about −88°) | the first squat and stand-up succeeded in 4 of 4 runs; a second squat in the same run failed (2 of 2): each cycle leaves the body about 2 cm further back |

## Hip pitch extension stop

An exact triangle-mesh sweep of the detailed CAD parts (FCL, then boolean overlap with manifold3d) finds two contacts when
the thigh swings **backward** (hip1 −). Both are between the hip connector bracket (`link_002_004` left,
`lower_body_link_001_005` right) and brackets on the pelvis:

| Contact | Hip pitch | Largest overlap | Pelvis part |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | left −20..−22°, right −17.5..−20° | under 1 mm (0.2 × 0.3 × 0.9 mm), clear again from −25° | `lower_body_base_link_021` / `_020` |
| 2 | left −63.5..−95°, right −61..−95° | about 3 × 3 × 5 mm (4 mm³), clear again from −100° | `lower_body_base_link_024` / `base_link_025` |

The models limit hip1 extension to −17.5° (left) / −15° (right), 2° short of contact 1, which is being removed in the design.
Flexion is free to beyond 120° (Fusion limit 120°). These stops were first taken for the *forward* limit, on the
reversed model. MuJoCo and MoveIt never saw them: the parts are on the parent and child of the same joint, whose
contacts are filtered.

*(before the orientation fix)* Hip pitch used (ROS sim, `full_body_arms`, the default stance): walking 0.04 m/step up to 42.9° left / 45.7° right, 3 of 3
runs ran the whole 43 s; the 7 cm squat up to 47.7° / 52.5°, 2 of 2. The tracking policy (16 kg leg model) uses −15..+18°.

With the current hardware (contact 1 still there, about +17.5° / +15°) the WBC needs a straighter stance:
`knee_rad` −0.3 and `torso_pitch_rad` 0.16 (torso leans back 9°; the hip pitch drops by the same amount). In ROS that walked
3 of 3 runs with the hips at most 11.3° / 14.7°. The squat and the get-up cannot be done within that stop. A TSID
joint-position bound on the hip had no effect (the soft contacts let the QP "move" the stance foot), and hard contacts
made the QP infeasible.

`motor_control_hybrid/config/motors.yaml` still allows +1.57 rad, because the tracking policy node refuses a registry
whose limits differ from its training MJCF.

## After the orientation fix (2026-11-22)

ROS sim, `full_body_arms`, WBC (default stance, knee −52°): walking (stepping in place, then 0.04 m/step from 22 s) ran the
whole run in 3 of 3 runs (42 s, 1.59–1.67 m, lean at most 2° fore-aft / 4° sideways). The hip pitch used 8..40° and the
ankle 7..41°.

**Squat, investigated.** The ankle axes sit near the heel now, and the ankles give 17 Nm. The formula stance
(hip1 = ankle = −knee/2, torso upright) puts the CoM 3 cm ahead of them in the model, and 5 cm in the sim because the
soft start-up ankle PD gives way. Squatting moved the knees and the CoM further forward, the ankles saturated, and the
robot tipped. Changes:
- the full-body launch leans the torso back 0.2 rad (`torso_pitch_rad`), which puts the stance CoM over the ankle axes;
  walking still passes 3 of 3 in ROS (42 s, hip 3..27°, ankle 7..46°);
- the squat's deep posture puts the CoM 2 cm ahead of the ankle axes (`squat_com_ref` = 'ankle'), and the robot stands back
  up into an IK stance with the CoM there, not into the start posture;
- the two IK solves are spread over about 12 cycles. In one cycle they took 44 ms in ROS, and the 30 ms cycle check stopped
  the controller (the "squat" run then just froze standing);
- the WBC accepts up to 10 mN·m over a torque bound as round-off; 3 mN·m on a saturated ankle had faulted.

The squat now goes down and comes back up offline, but **about 1 s after standing up the robot still tips forward**. Joint
PD alone cannot hold the CoM against 17 Nm ankles: there is no CoM feedback while both feet are flat, the same gap that keeps
the WBC from standing still. Fixing it needs a CoM/CoP balance loop for double support.
